# Clothing & PPE

Wear:
- Long sleeves
- Thick pants or overalls
- Bandana/hat
- Heavy shoes that can get wet
If you have the following please bring it, if not we have some spares :
- Thick work gloves
- Eye protection
- Ear plugs
- Face mask (N95 or respirator)
- Work apron
- Sunscreen

Using the angle grinder with the wire brush can spit sharp little wires at you, so keep your skin and especially face covered! Heavier/thicker sleeves are best.
# Stations

## Chemical Cleaning

Corners and feet soak in the acid bath, get scrubbed off and rinsed, and get scrubbed in soapy water and rinsed before sealing. 
### Acid Bath

#### Setup
![[PXL_20260818_160519936.jpg|403]]
![[PXL_20260819_023358221.jpg|406]]
Uncover the acid bath and give it a stir with the wood stick behind the tub. ~10 feet should be left in the bath overnight and ready to remove and scrub. 
![[PXL_20260819_023413416.jpg|408]]
If the feet are going slowly, consider adding more acid - add ~1 cup from the barrel to the bath. Be careful not to get moisture in the barrel and put the lid on when not in use.

Use the kettle in the kitchen area to add some hot water to the acid bath to help the powder dissolve and speed the cleaning process. Agitating the water occasionally will also help speed the process
#### Removing Pieces from the Acid Bath 
Use the long, thick dish gloves when reaching into the acid bath, and pull them up your arm if reaching all the way in - they're not quite long enough to reach the bottom of the bath. If your gloves flood with acid, don't panic! Just rinse them and your skin with clean water. 
#### Adding Pieces to the Acid Bath
Corners/feet should soak in the acid for 90 minutes up to overnight. 

### First Scrub

#### Setup
![[PXL_20260819_023320637.jpg|412]]

Stand the black plastic pallet container on its end.

Take corners/feet out of the acid bath (see [[#Removing Pieces from the Acid Bath]] ) and stand them up on the cinderblocks - any that don't fit on the cinderblocks can go on the ground. Set up ~3 corners on top of the pallet to be scrubbed. 

Put a 5-gallon bucket next to the pallet and fill with clean water for rinsing. Hose is in the warehouse by the rolling door. 

Set up tools on top of the pallet.
##### Tools:
- Cup for rinse water
- Deburring tool
- Razor blade scraper
- Scrub brush
- Bottle brush

Rinse the corners with clean water and scrub the gunk off the outside with a plastic scrub brush. Use the razor blade scraper to remove any stickers, tape, or adhesive. Don't go overboard on the scrubbing - the acid bath should be doing the work.

Use the bottle brush to scrub out the inside of each tube. When pulling the bottle brush out of the tube be careful - cup your other hand over the end to keep from wearing the gross water, and be aware of folks walking by on the sidewalk. Sometimes the edges of the tube are very thin and sharp after coming out of the acid bath - use the deburring tool to remove any sharp edges or swarf. Rinse the inside of the tube and move each piece to the soap scrub bins.
### Soap Scrub
#### Setup
![[PXL_20260818_161102270.jpg|412]]![[PXL_20260819_023343268.jpg|414]]

- only need to be in soap for 5-10 min
- set up & tear down daily
- ~2 tbsp per tub, fill 4/5 full w/ water
- Use other set of scrubbers in this station - just removing soap scum, don't worry about scrubbing off grime at this stage. 
- Once you've rinsed-scrubbed-rinsed, set upside down on a cinderblock or bucket. Move to drying once no longer dripping to fully dry (at night this will take longer, you might need to swing them around or towel dry them. Less of an issue for feet than corners)
- Feet & corners get painted on the paper covered tables. If the paper is torn, replace it (some below the clothing rack rn).
- Painting - think painting your nails. Thick enough coat that it fully saturates. Very sensitive to temp - runny in the heat of the day, globs up and will resist areas in the cool night.
- Painting the top surface of the feet is easiest, anything with a vertical surface will run in the heat of the day
- [!todo] get pictures of the feet painting process. Don't paint them to the table. Whit has a Process.
- Cure time depends on temp - 10 minutes to 45 minutes until can be handled (not tacky)
- Once handleable set them upright on the buckets between the shipping container and the building to get the full cure time (3-5 days)
- DO NOT stack painted metal until 3-5 days of cure time (they will bond to each other)
- When sealing bars, do a minimum of 4 at a time. Paint the top surface of each and then rotate them - it will give them enough time to cure so they don't drip.
- Make sure to get about an inch into the inside of the bars as well
- 20-45 min. to handleability, don't stack until 3-5 days are up. Also don't press the bars together until they're cured enough

## Angle Grinder

The beams and cross bars get cleaned up with a wire brush on an angle grinder.

- Up to two people at a sawhorse station
- Stack metal to adjust height for ergonomics - do what's best for your body. Remember you have knees - bend them! Don't hold the grinder in a death grip. 
- USE YOUR PPE
- We have thick & thin gloves, figure out what works best for your body and feel free to mix and match 
- Thin gloves don't have enough protection on their backs to stop wires
- Thick gloves are designed to reduce the vibration that can cause injury over time
- Grind the corner edge first ([!todo] get pictures of this) so it doesn't cause a sharp line. Do the surfaces after to blend the edges in. Do the edge closest to you so you don't have to reach over the bar to grind.
- Let the grinder do the work, don't press into it. The closer to parallel your brush is with the beam, the more surface area you can cover and the less the brush will spit and wear out. To get into corners and in more marred areas, you may need to increase the angle. 
- It will take multiple passes on a side, and you'll get faster as you go and get a feel for it
- We're not going for mirror smooth, we want pleasantly weathered and smooth feeling
- primary goal is rust off, secondary is to get it smooth
- Make sure you get the end caps
- If it's a crossbar, use sandpaper on the inside
