Wear gloves while working with the sealant, and be aware that it will eat through nitrile gloves if it stays on them too long.

Bars get coated on the stack of bars in the side lot next to the shipping containers. Use a drop cloth and scrap fabric to prevent drips of sealant on the last layer of bars. The chucks on top of the stack are covered in packing tape to help keep the bars from sticking to them.

Give the bars a quick wipe down with a dry rag before starting if they are visibly dusty.

When applying the sealant, think painting your nails. You want a thick enough coat that it can fully and evenly cover the face you're painting. The sealant is very sensitive to temperature - it will become very runny in the heat of the day and thicken and be harder to spread in the evenings as it cools down. 

Temperature also affects the cure time of the sealant - it can take anywhere between 10 and 45 minutes until the sealed bars are no longer tacky, at which point they're cured enough to be handled. The full cure time is 3-5 days. DO NOT stack sealed metal until 3-5 days of cure time have passed, or they will bond to each other. 

Paint bars in groups of at least 4 at a time, and paint the top surface of each. This will give each bar enough time to cure before being rotated, so the sealant doesn't run off or drip. Handle the bars from the inside while they are being painted. Once all for sides have been painted, seal about an inch into the inside of the bars.

Use a foam brush to apply the sealant. Dip just the corner of the brush into the sealant and press against the side of the can to squeeze out any excess. Dot sealant on along the first foot or so of the top face, then spread it out using the corner of your brush. Then coat the edge facing you along the area you just coated. Keep an eye out for any drips as you go - catching any drips down the sides by just wiping them upward with a gloved finger prevents them from curing into hard streaks later.
